"AI-powered" is on almost every trading product now. Some of these tools are useful. Some are ordinary software with a new label. A few are outright scams.

This post will not tell you which product to buy. It will help you judge any tool on your own.

## What "AI" usually means in trading

The term covers very different things:

- **Machine learning models** that look for patterns in price, volume or other data.
- **Large language models** that read and summarize text, such as filings, earnings calls or news.
- **Rule-based bots** that execute a fixed set of instructions. These are often called AI in marketing, even when no learning is involved.

Ask which one you are looking at. A rule-based bot that follows a clear strategy can be perfectly useful. It just is not learning anything. Knowing the difference helps you judge the claims.

## Where these tools can genuinely help

**Handling volume.** Software can scan thousands of stocks against your criteria faster than you can scan ten. That saves time. It does not mean the criteria are any good.

**Reading and summarizing.** A language model can pull key points from a long filing or a call transcript. That is a real time-saver. But you still need to check the original. More on that below.

**Consistent execution.** A bot follows its rules every time. It does not get scared after three losses or greedy after three wins. For traders whose main problem is discipline, automation can help.

**Faster research loops.** Tools can speed up testing an idea against historical data. Speed is good, as long as you test honestly.

Notice the pattern. These tools help you do the work faster. They do not supply the edge. If your strategy has no edge, automating it just loses money more efficiently.

## Where they fall short

**Predicting short-term prices.** Markets react to earnings surprises, policy decisions, geopolitics and crowd psychology. Patterns that worked in the past can fade once enough people trade them. Be very skeptical of any tool that claims to know where a price is going.

**Overfitting.** This is the big one. If you test enough variations of a strategy on the same data, some will look great by pure chance. Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu explained this in a 2014 paper on backtest overfitting. They showed that a strong backtest is easy to produce with enough trials, and that it can tell you little about future results. AI tools can try millions of variations. That makes the problem worse, not better.

**Confident mistakes.** Language models can produce wrong facts that sound right. The SEC, NASAA and FINRA warn about this directly. Their joint alert says AI can generate and spread false or inaccurate information. They advise you to confirm the underlying sources and review multiple sources before you invest.

**Black boxes.** If a tool cannot explain why it took a trade, you cannot tell whether its logic still applies when markets change.

## Red flags regulators warn about

In January 2024 the SEC's Office of Investor Education and Advocacy, NASAA and FINRA issued a joint investor alert on AI and investment fraud. Key warnings include:

- **Unregistered platforms claiming to use AI.** The alert gives examples of pitches promising AI that picks guaranteed stock winners.
- **Guaranteed returns.** The alert says to be wary of claims, even from registered firms, that AI can guarantee amazing returns.
- **Deepfakes.** Scammers can use AI to clone voices, fake videos or build realistic websites. They may impersonate executives, government staff or people you know.
- **Hype over substance.** For companies promoting AI, compare how much they spend on promotion versus building the business.

If a pitch sounds too good to be true, treat it that way.

## How to evaluate any AI trading tool

Run through these questions before you trust one with money:

1. **Who is behind it?** If someone is giving you investment advice or handling your money, check whether they are registered. FINRA BrokerCheck and the SEC's adviser search are free.
2. **What does it actually do?** Can they explain the method in plain words? "Proprietary AI" is not an answer.
3. **What is the real track record?** Ask for live or out-of-sample results, after fees and slippage. A backtest alone is not a track record.
4. **How many versions did they test?** A strategy chosen from thousands of trials is likely overfit.
5. **What happens in a bad market?** Ask for the worst drawdown and how long recovery took.
6. **What access does it need?** Be careful about giving any tool permission to trade or withdraw from your account.
7. **Can you start small?** Paper trade or use a tiny amount first. Compare its live results to what was promised.

## Using AI tools well

The best use of these tools is as an assistant, not an oracle.

- Let them speed up research. Verify anything important against the original source.
- Let them execute rules you understand and have tested yourself.
- Keep your own records so you can measure results honestly.
- Keep position sizes small until the tool proves itself live.

You stay responsible for every trade. A tool can make you faster. Only a tested process gives you an edge.
