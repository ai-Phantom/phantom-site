<p>An option is a contract that gives you the <em>right but not the obligation</em> to buy or sell 100 shares of a stock at a specific price (the strike price) before or on a specific date (the expiration date). You pay a premium for this right. That premium is your maximum possible loss on a long option — a feature that makes options one of the most powerful risk management tools available.</p>
        <h2>Calls and Puts</h2>
        <p>There are exactly two types of options:</p>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>Call option</strong> — gives you the right to BUY 100 shares at the strike price. You profit if the stock rises above your strike plus the premium you paid. Example: Buy 1 AAPL $220 call for $3.50 ($350 total). If AAPL rises to $230, your call is worth at least $10 ($1,000). A $650 gain on a $350 investment in the same time a stock buyer made $1,000 on $22,000. The leverage is real.</li>
          <li><strong>Put option</strong> — gives you the right to SELL 100 shares at the strike price. You profit if the stock falls below your strike minus the premium. The mirror image of a call — used to speculate on decline or to hedge an existing long position.</li>
        </ul>
        <h2>Why Options Exist</h2>
        <p>Options were originally created for hedging, not speculation. A farmer selling wheat in December wants to lock in today's price. An airline wants to cap its fuel costs six months out. An institutional investor holding $500M of AAPL wants to protect against a 20% decline without selling. Options solve all of these problems. Speculation came later.</p>
        <h2>The Risk Profile Asymmetry</h2>
        <p>The single most important property of long options: <strong>maximum loss is limited to the premium paid, maximum gain is theoretically unlimited (for calls)</strong>. This asymmetry — where you can lose $350 but potentially gain $5,000 — is why options are so powerful when used correctly, and why they expire worthless 70%+ of the time when used as lottery tickets.</p>
        <div class="course-callout gold"><strong>The real lesson:</strong> Options are not lottery tickets. Used correctly — as directional leveraged bets with defined risk, or as income-generating instruments through selling — they are among the most sophisticated tools available to individual traders.</div>
