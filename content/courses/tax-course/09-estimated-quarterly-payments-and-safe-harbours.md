## Why traders get this wrong

Wages come with withholding, so most employees never think about paying tax during the year. Trading gains come with nothing withheld, and the Code expects you to pay as you go. IRC section 6654 imposes an addition to tax, which everyone calls the penalty, when your estimated payments and withholding fall short of a required annual payment by the installment due dates. The penalty is an interest charge rather than a fine, but it compounds a mistake that is easy to avoid, because the safe harbours let you fix the required amount in April from numbers you already have.

## Who must pay, and how much

Form 1040-ES (2026) states the general rule. You must make estimated payments for 2026 if both apply: you expect to owe at least $1,000 for 2026 after subtracting withholding and refundable credits; and you expect withholding and credits to be less than the smaller of 90% of the tax to be shown on your 2026 return or 100% of the tax shown on your 2025 return, which must have covered all twelve months. Section 6654(d)(1)(C) and the form's Special Rules change the second figure to 110% of the 2025 tax if your 2025 adjusted gross income exceeded $150,000 ($75,000 if married filing separately for 2026). Farmers and fishermen substitute 66⅔% for 90%.

The smaller of those two numbers is the required annual payment. Section 6654(e) then adds two exceptions: no penalty if the tax on the return, after withholding, is under $1,000; and no penalty if you had no tax liability for the prior twelve-month year and were a U.S. citizen or resident throughout it.

The prior-year safe harbour is what makes this manageable for a trader. Whatever 2026 brings, paying 100% (or 110%) of the 2025 tax in four equal installments removes the penalty entirely; any balance is simply due with the return on April 15, 2027, interest-free until then. The price is that a bad 2026 following a good 2025 means paying in more than you owe and waiting for the refund; the annualized method below is the remedy.

## Due dates

Form 1040-ES (2026) lists the installments: April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; and January 15, 2027. They are not quarters: the second covers two months and the fourth four. You may skip the January 15, 2027 payment if you file the 2026 return and pay the balance by February 1, 2027. Pay through IRS Direct Pay, EFTPS or the vouchers in the package; a 2025 overpayment applied to 2026 counts as paid on April 15, 2026.

## The penalty arithmetic

Section 6654(a) computes the addition on each underpaid installment at the section 6621 underpayment rate for the period from the due date to the earlier of the date paid or the return due date. The rate is the federal short-term rate plus three percentage points, set quarterly; the IRS quarterly interest rates page shows 7% for every quarter of 2025 and, for 2026, 7% for January to March, 6% for April to June, 7% for July to September and 7% for October to December. Form 2210 does the calculation, or the IRS will compute it and bill you if you leave the form off. Two provisions soften it. Section 6654(g) treats tax withheld from wages as paid in equal parts on each installment date regardless of when it was withheld, so a trader with a day job can cure an early-year shortfall by raising W-4 withholding late in the year. And section 6654(d)(2), the annualized income installment method, lets each installment be computed on income actually received through the end of its period, on Form 2210 Schedule AI, so that gains realised in November are not penalised as if earned in March.

## Worked example

Single trader, 2025 return: total tax $38,000, adjusted gross income $210,000. For 2026 the trader expects tax of $60,000 on a better year. All figures are federal.

Safe harbour test for 2026. 90% of the expected 2026 tax = 0.90 × $60,000 = $54,000. Prior-year figure: AGI exceeded $150,000, so 110% of $38,000 = $41,800. Required annual payment = the smaller = $41,800. Installments = $41,800 ÷ 4 = $10,450 due April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027. The remaining $60,000 − $41,800 = $18,200 is due with the return by April 15, 2027 with no penalty and no interest, whatever the timing of the gains.

Missed installment. Suppose the June 15 payment is made on September 15 with the third installment. The underpayment of $10,450 runs 92 days: 16 days in the second quarter at 6% and 76 days in the third quarter at 7%. Penalty = $10,450 × 0.06 × 16 ÷ 365 + $10,450 × 0.07 × 76 ÷ 365 = $27.48 + $152.31 = $179.79. Small, but it accrues on every dollar and every day, and a trader who pays nothing until April 2027 on $41,800 of required installments owes roughly a year's interest on the average balance.

Annualized alternative. If the trader's 2026 income through May 31 was only wages, Schedule AI would show a lower required installment for the first two periods, and the same $41,800 could be back-loaded to September and January without penalty. The method must be used consistently for all four periods and Form 2210 must be filed with the return.

## Paying through withholding instead

If you also have wages, withholding is usually the cleaner tool. Section 6654(g) treats income tax withheld from wages as paid in equal parts on the four due dates unless you show otherwise, so withholding added by a new Form W-4 in November counts as if a quarter of it had been paid on April 15. A trader who discovers in the autumn that the year's gains have outrun the plan can raise withholding for the remaining pay periods and repair the earlier installments retroactively, which a late estimated payment cannot do: an estimated payment is credited only on the day it is made. The same equal-parts rule applies to tax withheld from an IRA distribution. Withholding does not change the required annual payment; it only changes how and when the IRS treats you as having paid it.

## What to do each April

Take last year's Form 1040: line 24 total tax and line 11 adjusted gross income. If AGI exceeded $150,000, multiply the tax by 1.10; otherwise by 1.00. Subtract the withholding you expect this year, divide the remainder by four, and schedule the four payments. If the current year looks materially worse, switch to 90% of a current-year projection, revisited each quarter, or use Schedule AI. Keep the confirmation numbers; Form 2210 and the IRS transcript are where a dispute over a missing payment is settled. State estimated payments have their own dates and rules and are outside this course.
