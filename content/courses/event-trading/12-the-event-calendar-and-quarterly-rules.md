## The calendar is the position

Everything in this course begins with knowing when. A trader who does not know that the FOMC statement is at 2:00 p.m. on December 9 will hold a short-gamma index position through it by accident. A trader who does not know NVIDIA reports on November 17 will sell a weekly the Friday before and wonder why the premium was so rich. The calendar is not administration; it is the first risk control, and it has to be built from primary sources, on a fixed schedule, before the quarter starts.

This lesson builds the fourth-quarter 2026 calendar as a worked example, then sets out how to write rules for the quarter that the journal can evaluate afterward.

## The sources, and when to check them

- **Federal Reserve, FOMC calendar.** Published for the whole year, updated if a meeting is rescheduled. Check once a quarter.
- **BLS release schedules** for CPI and the Employment Situation. Published for the year. Check once a quarter; a government shutdown can move them, so re-check if one is in the news.
- **Company investor relations pages** for earnings dates. Companies confirm two to four weeks ahead by press release. Check weekly for the next two weeks' reporters; a projected date on a screener is not a confirmed date.
- **S&P Dow Jones Indices press releases.** Quarterly rebalance announcements come about two weeks before the third Friday; ad hoc changes come with three to five trading days' notice. Check twice a week in the two weeks before a quarter-end Friday.
- **FTSE Russell reconstitution schedule.** Published months ahead; the preliminary lists appear a few weeks before the effective date.
- **Exchange holiday calendars.** Half days change what "after the close" means.

## Worked example

Build the calendar for October 1 to December 31, 2026, for a trader holding SPY, IWM and NVDA. Count the trading days: October has 22, November 20 (Thanksgiving, November 26, is a holiday), December 22 (Christmas is a holiday), total **64**. Now the events, each from its primary source.

**Employment Situation (BLS schedule), 8:30 a.m. ET:** Friday October 2 (September data), Friday November 6 (October), Friday December 4 (November).

**CPI (BLS schedule), 8:30 a.m. ET:** Wednesday October 14 (September data), Tuesday November 10 (October), Thursday December 10 (November).

**FOMC (Federal Reserve calendar):** October 27–28, statement 2:00 p.m. on Wednesday October 28, press conference 2:30; December 8–9, statement 2:00 p.m. on Wednesday December 9 with the Summary of Economic Projections, press conference 2:30.

**NVIDIA (company):** third-quarter fiscal 2027 results, call set for Tuesday, November 17, after the close.

**FTSE Russell (schedule):** November semi-annual reconstitution rank day Friday October 30; effective after the close on Friday December 11.

**S&P Dow Jones Indices (methodology):** quarterly rebalance effective prior to the open on Monday December 21, meaning index funds trade the close on Friday December 18, which is also quarterly options expiration.

Distinct event dates: October 2, 14, 28, 30; November 6, 10, 17; December 4, 9, 10, 11, 18. That is **twelve** dates, of which rank day (October 30) has no direct price effect, so **eleven** market-moving dates out of 64 trading days: 11 / 64 = **17%**. Add the reaction days (the day after NVDA, the session after each 8:30 print is the same day) and it is about a fifth of the quarter.

Now the collisions. **December 9, 10, 11** is a three-day cluster: FOMC with projections on Wednesday, CPI on Thursday morning, Russell reconstitution at Friday's close. An IWM position carries all three, and the first two are index-wide vol events with the third a liquidity event in that exact product. **November 17** falls a week after CPI and eleven days before the Thanksgiving half-session; the NVDA weekly expiring November 20 is the one lesson 4's term structure already showed at 36.5% vol two months out. **October 28 and 30** put the FOMC statement two days before Russell rank day, which matters only if you hold names near the Russell 1000/2000 cutoff.

## Writing the quarter's rules

A rule for the quarter is a pre-commitment about what you will and will not do on the dates above. It is written before October 1, it is specific enough that the journal can score it as followed or not, and it is not changed until January. Five examples, each derived from a lesson in this course:

1. **No short-gamma index option position open at 2:00 p.m. on October 28 or December 9** unless it was sized for a 1.5% move within the hour (lesson 6). Positions are closed or rolled by 1:30 p.m.
2. **Every NVDA position held through November 17 is sized by the gap rule** at two implied moves, with the implied move read from the November 20 straddle on November 17 and written in the journal before 4:00 p.m. (lessons 2 and 10). No extended-hours stops.
3. **No new positions in the 8:30 bar** on the six BLS dates. The regular-session plan, written the night before, names what is done at 9:30 under a beat, a miss, and in-line, and the first hour decides which applies (lesson 7).
4. **The December 9–11 cluster is a reduced-size week for IWM:** half the normal position from the December 8 close to the December 14 open, and no short strangles on IWM expiring December 11 or 18 (lessons 4, 6, 8).
5. **Every event row is opened before the print and closed within two sessions of expiry**, and at the end of the quarter the "followed plan" column is tallied before any P&L is looked at (lesson 11).

Rules like these are not predictions. Nothing in them says which way any event will go. They are constraints on size, timing and record-keeping that remove the errors the course has shown to be avoidable: the wrong date, the unsized gap, the stop in the thin session, the market order into the 2:00 bar, the thesis written after the fact.

## The quarter-end review

On the last trading day of the quarter, before looking at P&L, answer four questions from the journal:

- How many event rows were opened before the print, and how many after? The second number should be zero.
- How many rules were broken, and what did those rows return relative to the rows where the rules held?
- What was the mean and standard deviation of implied minus realised across all rows, and does the sign match your structure choices?
- What is the running t-statistic on each structure with bounded loss, and how many rows short of a meaningful sample is it?

Then set the next quarter's calendar, from the sources, and write the next quarter's rules, amending only what the review showed was wrong, and only in writing. That loop, calendar, rules, journal, review, is the whole of event trading as a practice. The structures in lessons 2 through 8 are the vocabulary; the loop is the grammar.
