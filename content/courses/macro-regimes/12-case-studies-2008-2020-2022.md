## Three bear markets, three regimes

The three largest SPY declines since 2007 look alike on a price chart and nothing alike on the four axes. This lesson puts them side by side with dated data: what each axis read at the peak, at the trough and a year later, what the cross-asset complex did, and what the regime tools of the course would have told you at the time. The point is not the history. It is that a trader who read only price would have applied the same playbook to three different environments, and two of the three would have punished it.

## 2008: growth and credit

SPY's total-return peak was 2007-10-09; the trough was 2009-03-09, 517 days and -55.2% later. At the peak, the funds rate was 4.91%, the 2s10s spread +0.52 (the curve had un-inverted in June after eighteen months negative), the 3m10y +0.61, CPI inflation 3.6%, the 10-year real yield +2.34%, and the Fed's balance sheet $0.87 trillion. At the trough, the funds rate was 0.20%, 2s10s +1.93, 3m10y +2.66, CPI inflation -0.4% (deflation), the real yield +2.06%, the balance sheet $1.90 trillion. The VIX peaked at 80.86 on 2008-11-20; the worst session was 2008-10-15 at -9.8%.

The cross-asset picture is the classic growth shock. Over the decline TLT returned +25.1%, GLD +23.9%, USO -53.3%, the dollar +13.3%, HYG -32.9%. Bonds and gold were the hedge; oil and credit fell with equities; the dollar rose as a funding currency. The regime tools fired early: the 200-day cross on 2007-11-08 at -6.0% from the peak, the VIX above 30 on 2007-11-12. The switching rule was out of the market for a -34.8% move. In the twelve months from the trough, SPY returned +71.9%, TLT -9.9%, GLD +21.1%, and the balance sheet, which had gone from $0.93 trillion on 2008-09-10 to $2.25 trillion on 2008-12-17, was the mechanism that ended it.

## 2020: growth and liquidity, compressed

The peak was 2020-02-19; the trough 2020-03-23, 33 days and -33.7% later. At the peak: funds rate 1.59%, 2s10s +0.14, 3m10y -0.02 (the 3-month curve had been inverted on and off since March 2019), CPI 2.3%, real yield -0.09%, balance sheet $4.17 trillion. At the trough: funds rate 0.15% (the range was cut to zero on 2020-03-15), 2s10s +0.48, 3m10y +0.74, CPI 1.5%, real yield -0.04%, balance sheet $4.67 trillion and rising at a pace never seen before: $7.17 trillion by 2020-06-10. The VIX peaked at 82.69 on 2020-03-16, the same day as the worst session (-10.9%), a week before the price low.

Cross-asset: TLT +14.2%, GLD -3.6%, USO -56.4%, dollar +2.9%, HYG -21.9%. Bonds hedged; gold did not, because the liquidation was indiscriminate in the final week; credit fell hard and was the market the Fed intervened in directly. The regime tools were late in price terms because the decline was so fast: the 200-day cross on 2020-02-27 at -12.1%, the VIX above 30 the same day. The switching rule was out for a +1.1% move: it missed the worst of the fall and the first third of the recovery in equal measure. Twelve months from the trough: SPY +77.5%, TLT -16.0%, GLD +10.6%.

The sequence matters. The VIX and the worst day came first; the Fed's open-ended purchase announcement came on 2020-03-23, which was the price low; the balance sheet data confirming the expansion arrived over the following weeks, by which time SPY had rebounded 31.2% (to 2020-05-08, the day the VIX fell back below 30). A trader waiting for the liquidity axis to confirm would have re-entered after the rebound.

## 2022: inflation and rates

The peak was 2022-01-03; the trough 2022-10-12, 282 days and -24.5% later. At the peak: funds rate 0.08%, 2s10s +0.85, 3m10y +1.55, CPI for the month 7.6%, real yield -0.97%, balance sheet $8.76 trillion (it would peak at $8.97 trillion on 2022-04-13), 10-year breakeven 2.60%. At the trough: funds rate 3.08% (hiked from 0.08% during the decline; it would reach 4.33% by year-end and 5.33% by July 2023), 2s10s -0.37 (inverted since July), 3m10y +0.21, CPI for the month 7.8%, real yield +1.62%, balance sheet $8.76 trillion and falling. The VIX peaked at only 36.45, on 2022-03-07; the worst session was -4.3% on 2022-09-13.

Cross-asset: TLT -29.3%, GLD -7.3%, USO +29.4%, dollar +17.7%, HYG -14.6%, LQD's price down more than HYG's. Nothing hedged except the dollar and oil. The regime tools fired on time in price terms (the 200-day cross on 2022-01-21 at -8.3%, the VIX above 30 on 2022-01-25) but the switching rule saved only 5.8% because the decline oscillated around the average all year. Twelve months from the trough: SPY +23.5%, TLT -11.5%, GLD +11.1%. The inflation data did not turn until after the price did: CPI for October 2022, the trough month, was higher than for January.

## What the three have in common and what they do not

All three show the same lag structure: the VIX and the price move first, the trend filter fires 6 to 22 sessions later, the macro data confirms months later, and the recovery begins before any of the slow variables have turned. All three had the 200-day filter out of the market within a month of the peak. And in all three, TLT lost money in the year after the trough.

What differs is the direction of policy and, with it, the behaviour of bonds and gold during the decline. In 2008 and 2020 the Fed cut and expanded; bonds rallied, and the stock-bond correlation was negative. In 2022 the Fed hiked and shrank; bonds fell harder than stocks, and the correlation turned positive. The single most useful thing to know about a bear market, for a trader deciding what to hold against it, is which way the funds rate is moving while it happens, and that is readable from FRED every day.

## Worked example

Data: SPY, TLT, GLD, USO, HYG adjusted closes and `DX-Y.NYB`, `^VIX` closes from the Yahoo Finance chart API, current session dropped; FRED DFF, T10Y2Y, T10Y3M, CPIAUCSL, DFII10, WALCL, T10YIE. Peak and trough are SPY's highest adjusted close before the decline and lowest during it. Each asset's return over the decline is its close on the trough date divided by its close on the peak date, minus one; FRED readings are the last observation on or before each date; CPI year-on-year is for the calendar month containing the date, computed by dividing by the observation twelve months earlier; WALCL is the last Wednesday on or before the date. The 12-month return is from the trough date to the nearest session on or before the date 365 days later.

2008: SPY -55.2%, TLT +25.1%, GLD +23.9%, USO -53.3%, DXY +13.3%, HYG -32.9%. VIX peak 80.86 (2008-11-20); worst day -9.8% (2008-10-15). DFF 4.91 to 0.20; 2s10s +0.52 to +1.93; 3m10y +0.61 to +2.66; CPI 3.6% to -0.4%; DFII10 +2.34 to +2.06; WALCL $0.87T to $1.90T. Twelve months after: SPY +71.9%, TLT -9.9%, GLD +21.1%.

2020: SPY -33.7%, TLT +14.2%, GLD -3.6%, USO -56.4%, DXY +2.9%, HYG -21.9%. VIX peak 82.69 (2020-03-16); worst day -10.9% (2020-03-16). DFF 1.59 to 0.15; 2s10s +0.14 to +0.48; 3m10y -0.02 to +0.74; CPI 2.3% to 1.5%; DFII10 -0.09 to -0.04; WALCL $4.17T to $4.67T, then $7.17T on 2020-06-10. Twelve months after: SPY +77.5%, TLT -16.0%, GLD +10.6%.

2022: SPY -24.5%, TLT -29.3%, GLD -7.3%, USO +29.4%, DXY +17.7%, HYG -14.6%. VIX peak 36.45 (2022-03-07); worst day -4.3% (2022-09-13). DFF 0.08 to 3.08 (4.33 on 2022-12-30; 5.33 on 2023-07-27); 2s10s +0.85 to -0.37; 3m10y +1.55 to +0.21; CPI 7.6% to 7.8%; DFII10 -0.97 to +1.62; WALCL $8.76T at both dates, peak $8.97T on 2022-04-13; T10YIE 2.60 at the peak, high of 3.02 on 2022-04-21. Twelve months after: SPY +23.5%, TLT -11.5%, GLD +11.1%.

Check on one figure: the 2022 real-yield change during the decline is 1.62 - (-0.97) = +2.59 points; over the calendar year (lesson 4) it was +2.62. Nearly the whole year's repricing happened inside the equity drawdown.
