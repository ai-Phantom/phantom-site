<p>You do not need 20 different patterns. You need 2–3 patterns you understand deeply and can execute flawlessly. These three setups account for the majority of high-probability momentum trades available on any given day.</p>
        <h2>Setup 1: The Bull Flag</h2>
        <p>The bull flag is the highest-probability momentum continuation pattern. Structure: a strong, nearly vertical move upward on high volume (the flagpole), followed by a tight, orderly consolidation on declining volume (the flag). The consolidation is a pause, not a reversal — profit-taking rather than distribution.</p>
        <p><strong>Entry:</strong> A break above the upper boundary of the consolidation on volume expanding back toward flagpole levels. <strong>Stop:</strong> Below the low of the flag consolidation. <strong>Target:</strong> Flagpole height added to the breakout point.</p>
        <h2>Setup 2: The Volume Breakout</h2>
        <p>Price consolidates in a defined range for weeks or months. Volume contracts as both buyers and sellers lose interest. Then, on a specific catalyst (earnings beat, product launch, analyst upgrade), price breaks above resistance on volume 2–3× the 20-day average. This is institutional accumulation made visible.</p>
        <p><strong>Entry:</strong> First candle close above resistance on 2× average volume, or a pullback to the breakout level within 1–3 days. <strong>Stop:</strong> Below the breakout level (old resistance becomes new support). <strong>Target:</strong> Width of the base added to the breakout point.</p>
        <h2>Setup 3: The Moving Average Reclaim</h2>
        <p>A stock that has been trending strongly pulls back to its 20-day or 50-day moving average on declining volume, then reclaims the MA on an expansion of volume. This is the trend reasserting itself after a healthy correction.</p>
        <p><strong>Entry:</strong> Candle that closes back above the MA after touching it intraday, ideally with volume confirmation. <strong>Stop:</strong> 1–2% below the MA. <strong>Target:</strong> Prior swing high or measured move.</p>
        <div class="course-callout gold"><strong>Key principle:</strong> All three setups share the same DNA — a base of consolidation followed by expansion. Contraction leads to expansion. The longer and tighter the consolidation, the more explosive the eventual move tends to be.</div>
