<p>Managing a $10,000 personal account and managing a $10,000,000 institutional portfolio are more similar than most people assume — and the differences are mostly in favour of the individual investor, not the institution. The institutional frameworks we cover in this course apply directly to any portfolio, any size.</p>
        <h2>What Institutions Do Differently</h2>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>They write everything down.</strong> Every investment decision is documented: the thesis, the expected outcome, the conditions under which the thesis would be wrong. This prevents post-hoc rationalisation and forces disciplined thinking.</li>
          <li><strong>They define risk before entry.</strong> Position sizing at institutions is driven by risk contribution, not conviction level. A position is sized so that if the thesis is wrong and the stop is hit, the portfolio loses a predetermined, acceptable amount.</li>
          <li><strong>They benchmark relative to an index.</strong> Success is not "did I make money?" but "did I make more than my benchmark on a risk-adjusted basis?" This reframes the entire decision-making process.</li>
          <li><strong>They separate alpha from beta.</strong> Beta is market exposure anyone can buy cheaply through an ETF. Alpha is skill-based return above that. Institutions are ruthlessly honest about how much of their return is beta vs genuine alpha.</li>
          <li><strong>They attribute performance systematically.</strong> Every quarter, they decompose returns: how much came from asset allocation, how much from security selection, how much from market timing.</li>
        </ul>
        <h2>Why These Frameworks Work for Individual Investors</h2>
        <p>Individual investors have real advantages: no mandate constraints, no career risk from unconventional positions, no redemption pressure forcing bad timing, no transaction cost of size. The institutional frameworks — documentation, risk budgeting, benchmarking, attribution — work even better when the friction of institutional constraints is removed.</p>
