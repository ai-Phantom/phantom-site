<p>Invented by Japanese rice trader Munehisa Homma in the 18th century, candlestick charts remain the most expressive visual language for price action. Each candle tells the story of a battle between buyers and sellers during a specific time period.</p>
        <h2>Anatomy of a Single Candle</h2>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>Body</strong> — the rectangle between the open and close price. A green (or white) body means price closed above where it opened — buyers won. A red (or black) body means price closed below the open — sellers won. A wide body = strong conviction. A narrow body = indecision.</li>
          <li><strong>Upper wick (shadow)</strong> — the line above the body, showing the highest price reached during the period. A long upper wick means buyers pushed price up but sellers rejected the move and pushed it back down before close. This is <em>bearish</em> pressure at that level.</li>
          <li><strong>Lower wick</strong> — the line below the body, showing the lowest price reached. A long lower wick means sellers pushed price down but buyers stepped in strongly and recovered most or all of the move before close. This is <em>bullish</em> support at that level.</li>
        </ul>
        <h2>The Five Most Important Candle Patterns</h2>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>Doji</strong> — Open and close are almost equal, creating a cross shape. Neither buyers nor sellers won. Signals indecision. Often precedes a reversal when it appears after a strong trend.</li>
          <li><strong>Hammer</strong> — Long lower wick (at least 2× the body), small body near the top. Appeared at the low of a downtrend. Buyers rejected the lows decisively. Bullish reversal signal.</li>
          <li><strong>Shooting Star</strong> — Long upper wick, small body near the bottom. Appears at the high of an uptrend. Sellers rejected the highs. Bearish reversal signal.</li>
          <li><strong>Marubozu</strong> — No wicks at all — the candle is pure body from open to close. Maximum conviction. A green marubozu means buyers controlled every tick of the session. Watch for these after consolidation.</li>
          <li><strong>Engulfing Pattern</strong> — A two-candle pattern where the second candle completely engulfs the first. A bullish engulfing (green after red) after a downtrend signals buyer takeover. One of the most reliable reversal signals in all of technical analysis.</li>
        </ul>
        <div class="course-callout blue"><strong>Practice exercise:</strong> Open any chart — set to daily candles. Spend 10 minutes identifying hammers, shooting stars, and dojis. Note every time one appears at a significant high or low. You will quickly see why these patterns have been traded for 250 years.</div>
