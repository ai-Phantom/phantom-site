<p>Technical analysis is the study of price and volume data to forecast future price direction. It rests on three foundational assumptions that have held across 200 years of market data: (1) price discounts all known information, (2) prices move in trends, and (3) history tends to repeat.</p>
        <h2>TA vs Fundamental Analysis</h2>
        <p>Fundamental analysis asks: <em>"What is this company worth?"</em> It examines earnings, revenue, competitive moats, and management quality. Technical analysis asks: <em>"Where is this price going next?"</em> It examines the chart — the result of every buy and sell decision by every participant.</p>
        <p>The two approaches are not in conflict. Most professional traders use both: fundamentals tell you <strong>what</strong> to buy, technicals tell you <strong>when</strong> to buy it. A great company bought at the wrong time (into a downtrend, at resistance) will underperform a mediocre company bought at the right time (at support, in an uptrend).</p>
        <h2>What the Chart Is Really Showing You</h2>
        <p>Every candle on a chart is the aggregated result of millions of decisions — institutional portfolio managers, algorithmic traders, retail investors, market makers all interacting in real time. The chart is not noise. It is compressed human psychology: greed, fear, conviction, and uncertainty rendered in price and volume.</p>
        <h2>The Limits</h2>
        <p>Technical analysis does not predict the future with certainty. It identifies <em>probabilities</em> — situations where price has historically tended to behave in specific ways. A technical setup that works 60% of the time with a 2:1 reward-to-risk ratio produces a positive expected value over hundreds of trades, even though it fails 40% of the time.</p>
