<p>If you understand only one concept from technical analysis, make it support and resistance. Every chart pattern, every entry and exit strategy, every stop-loss placement — all of it builds on this single foundational idea.</p>
        <h2>Why Certain Price Levels Matter</h2>
        <p>Support is a price level where buying interest is consistently strong enough to prevent further decline. Resistance is where selling pressure consistently prevents further advance. These levels persist because <strong>traders remember them</strong>.</p>
        <p>Consider a stock that bounced hard from $150 three times over the past year. Every trader watching that chart knows $150 is significant. When price approaches $150 again, buyers who missed the previous bounces step in early. Sellers who bought at $150 in the past feel relief at breaking even and sell. The level becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy — it works because enough traders act as if it will work.</p>
        <h2>How to Identify Real Levels</h2>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>Multiple touches</strong> — A level tested 3+ times without breaking is stronger than one tested once. Each test that holds adds credibility.</li>
          <li><strong>High volume at the level</strong> — Reversals from support or resistance on high volume are more significant. Volume is conviction.</li>
          <li><strong>Round numbers</strong> — $100, $150, $200 act as psychological support/resistance. Human brains anchor on round numbers and place orders there.</li>
          <li><strong>Previous highs and lows</strong> — Old highs become future resistance. Old lows become future support.</li>
        </ul>
        <h2>Role Reversal — The Most Reliable Pattern</h2>
        <p>When a support level breaks convincingly (price closes below it on high volume), that level typically becomes future resistance. When price rallies back to the old support, sellers who bought there now sell to break even — creating a ceiling exactly where the floor used to be. This role reversal is one of the most reliable, repeatable patterns in markets.</p>
        <div class="course-callout gold"><strong>The key rule:</strong> The more times a level has been tested without breaking, the stronger it becomes — and the more explosive the break when it finally happens. Strong support levels that eventually fail tend to produce the sharpest declines.</div>
