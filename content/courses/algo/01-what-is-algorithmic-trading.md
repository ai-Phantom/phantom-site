<p>Algorithmic trading means using computer programs to execute trading decisions automatically based on predefined rules. The rules can be simple (buy when the 50-day MA crosses above the 200-day MA) or extraordinarily complex (machine learning models processing alternative data). What they share: <strong>decisions are made by code, not by real-time human emotion</strong>.</p>
        <h2>Systematic vs Discretionary Trading</h2>
        <p><strong>Discretionary trading</strong> is what most traders do — they analyse charts, read news, form a view, and place trades based on judgment. This can work, but it is subject to emotional biases, inconsistency, and the physical limits of human attention.</p>
        <p><strong>Systematic (algorithmic) trading</strong> defines rules in advance and follows them mechanically. It cannot panic, get overconfident, or deviate from the rules. The tradeoff is that it also cannot adapt dynamically to genuinely novel market conditions.</p>
        <h2>What Algorithms Do Well</h2>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>Speed</strong> — Execution in microseconds, reacting to data faster than any human.</li>
          <li><strong>Consistency</strong> — Every signal generates the same response, every time, without hesitation.</li>
          <li><strong>Scale</strong> — Simultaneously monitoring hundreds of stocks, dozens of signals, and multiple timeframes.</li>
          <li><strong>Backtesting</strong> — Testing a strategy against years of historical data in seconds to understand its statistical properties.</li>
          <li><strong>Emotionlessness</strong> — No FOMO, no panic, no revenge trading. The system executes the plan.</li>
        </ul>
